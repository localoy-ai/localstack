# Directing: staging, performance, tone

The director decides what each moment is about and makes everything serve
it: where people stand, how they look, how long we stay.

## What is this scene about?

Before staging, say it in a few words: "she realises the voice is hers",
"he's lying". Every choice below serves that.

## Blocking and staging

- **Where people are** tells the relationship: close for intimacy or
  threat, far for distance, a barrier between them (a table, a door) for
  conflict.
- **Who's higher, who's centred, who's in the light** tells who has power.
- **Movement shows change**: a character crossing the frame toward
  another is a decision.
- **Stage in depth**: one in front, one behind, rather than both flat
  across the frame.
- **Entrances and exits**: enter late, exit on the turn.

## Performance in pictures

AI pictures act with face and body; direct them as you would an actor:

- **Name the feeling and the body**: "jaw clenched, eyes wet, holding
  back tears" beats "sad".
- **The reaction is often the shot**: the face hearing the news, not the
  news.
- **Eyelines**: say where they look (at the radio, past the camera, at
  the floor).
- **Hands** carry emotion (gripping, shaking, reaching); a close-up of
  hands can replace a line.
- **Less is stronger**: a small expression in a close-up reads more than a
  big one in a wide shot.

## Tone and genre

- **Thriller**: withhold; long holds before reveals; tight frames; cold
  light with one warm source; sound does the work.
- **Horror**: what's not shown; negative space; silence then a sound.
- **Comedy**: wider frames so we see the body; timing (hold a beat for the
  reaction); bright light.
- **Drama**: close on faces; patience; motivated light.
- **Ads**: clarity first; the product in light, in hand, in use.
- **Kids**: bright, clear, warm; one emotion at a time.

## Rhythm of a sequence

Alternate tension and release. After a big moment, give the viewer a beat
(a wide, a silence) before the next.

## What this means in VertX

- Staging and performance are in the `picture` prompt: positions,
  expressions, eyelines, hands.
- Holds and beats are clip lengths (`for`) and gaps in the voice.
- Use `look` on the key frames: does the face say the scene's sentence?
