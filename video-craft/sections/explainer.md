# Explainers and tutorials

Teaching on video: the viewer should understand something by the end. The
craft is clarity and pace for comprehension.

## Structure

- Start with the question or the outcome ("By the end you'll know…").
- One idea per scene; each builds on the last; recap at the end.
- An analogy for the hard idea, shown, not just said.
- 120–150 words a minute of narration; slower for dense ideas.

## Showing ideas

- Build diagrams in the order they're explained (see motion-design).
- Use the same visual for the same thing every time it appears.
- Highlight what's being talked about; dim the rest.
- Numbers as single animated figures or simple bars, labelled directly.

## Screen tutorials

- Record at a size where text reads after cropping; one task per clip.
- Zoom to where the action is (cursor, field, button) and back out to
  orient; never leave a viewer hunting for the cursor.
- Slow, deliberate moves while recording; pause on each result.
- Cut waiting (loading, typing long text); keep the clicks.
- Label each step with a lower third or a number.

## Pace

- Hold a new diagram for a beat before explaining it.
- Pause after a key point (0.5–1 s of nothing new).
- Fast enough to keep attention, slow enough to follow: when in doubt,
  cut words, not pauses.

## What this means in VertX

- `record` and `tour` for screens; `keys` (scale and centre) to zoom to the
  action; `scene` for diagrams and data; `captions` for every voice.
