# Rights and care

What you make goes out under the person's name. Keep it theirs to post.

## Music

- **Use**: music VertX makes, music the person owns or made, or music
  licensed for this use (a library licence that covers social and
  commercial use when it's an ad).
- **Don't**: rip a song, use a popular track in an ad, or count on a
  platform's in-app music library when the video is posted elsewhere
  (those licences usually cover only that platform, and often not ads).
- Platforms detect copyrighted music and mute, block or take the money.

## Pictures and footage

- Use what the person owns, what the models make, or what's licensed.
- Don't recreate a famous artwork, character or logo that isn't theirs.
- Brand assets: only the person's own, as their DESIGN.md says.

## People: faces and voices

- **No real person's face or voice** without their consent: no
  lookalikes of celebrities or public figures, no voice imitation.
- **The person themselves**: their own photo or voice, with their say-so,
  is fine.
- **Nothing that puts words in a real person's mouth**, real or
  synthetic.
- **Children**: never real children's likeness without a parent's
  consent; keep fictional children in safe, ordinary situations.

## Truth and labels

- **Don't pass AI pictures off as real footage of real events**, real
  places at a real moment, or real people.
- **Platforms ask for labels** on realistic AI-made or altered content
  (YouTube, TikTok and Meta all have AI-content labels; check each one's
  current rule when posting realistic material). Stylised work (comic,
  cartoon) usually doesn't need one; realistic people and events do.
- Documentary illustration: say on screen it's an illustration or a
  reconstruction.

## Claims

- Ads: only claims the person can back (their numbers, their reviews).
  No invented testimonials, prices, results or "doctors recommend".
- Health, money, legal topics: no promises; say it's general information.

## Credits

- Credit the author when adapting their work, guests in a podcast,
  licensed music when the licence asks.

## What this means for the job

- The brief's "must avoid" and the cast phase catch most of this early.
- When a request needs something you can't use (a hit song, a celebrity's
  face), say so and offer the honest version (music in that style, an
  original character).
